Seattle's pay floor for app-based workers

Seattle's App-Based Worker Minimum Payment Ordinance (SMC 8.37) sets a minimum for work done through apps — food delivery included — inside the city. Unlike California's Prop 22, which is checked over a whole pay period, Seattle's standard is figured per offer. Here it is in plain language, with 2026 numbers.

The formula (2026)

Minimum pay per offer = whichever is greater:
($0.47 × engaged minutes + $0.80 × engaged miles)  or  $5.34

The per-minute and per-mile amounts are set by the ordinance and adjusted annually; the figures above are the Seattle Office of Labor Standards (OLS) amounts for 2026. "Engaged" time and miles run from when you accept an offer through completing it — time spent online waiting for an offer isn't engaged time. As with the other floors on this site, the minimum is figured on the company's pay: tips are yours on top and don't count toward meeting it.

Why "per offer" changes the math

Because the test applies to each offer separately, a long, well-paid delivery can't legally "cover" a short one that fell under the floor. The $5.34 per-offer minimum exists for exactly those short offers: even a five-minute hop has a floor. When you sanity-check your own pay, check offer by offer first — a weekly average can hide individual offers below the standard.

Worked examples (2026 rates)

A typical delivery — 30 engaged minutes, 8 engaged miles, 1 offer:

  • Time part: $0.47 × 30 = $14.10
  • Mileage part: $0.80 × 8 = $6.40
  • Formula total: $20.50 — above the $5.34 minimum, so the floor for this offer is $20.50

A short hop — 5 engaged minutes, 1 engaged mile, 1 offer:

  • Time part: $0.47 × 5 = $2.35
  • Mileage part: $0.80 × 1 = $0.80
  • Formula total: $3.15 — below the per-offer minimum, so the floor is $5.34

A whole block, as an approximation — 10 offers, 200 engaged minutes, 60 engaged miles: the formula total is ($0.47 × 200) + ($0.80 × 60) = $94.00 + $48.00 = $142.00, well above 10 × $5.34 = $53.40. Treat block checks like this as a rough screen only: the ordinance's real test is per offer, so a block above the floor doesn't prove every offer in it cleared.

Who it covers — and who it doesn't

The ordinance covers app-based work performed in Seattle, including food and grocery delivery through the major platforms. It is a City of Seattle rule, enforced by the Office of Labor Standards. Two neighbours worth knowing about:

  • Rideshare is priced separately. Passenger trips run under Washington State's TNC minimum-compensation law (RCW 49.46.300), which pays more for trips starting in Seattle than for trips elsewhere in the state. The full state figures are in the table on our methodology page, and the calculator's floor check has options for both.
  • Outside Seattle city limits, this ordinance doesn't apply — suburban deliveries a few minutes away can be under a different (or no) floor. Which side of the city line an offer starts on matters.

How to sanity-check your own pay

  1. Pick a few recent offers in your app and note each one's engaged minutes, engaged miles, and pay before tips.
  2. Run each through the formula above — or use the check below — and compare.
  3. If individual offers repeatedly land below the floor with no adjustment visible on your statements, the Seattle Office of Labor Standards is the agency that enforces the ordinance and takes worker complaints.

Check a block of your own work: the pay-floor check on the calculator runs the Seattle formula with your engaged minutes, engaged miles, and offer count — pick "Seattle, WA — app-based work (incl. delivery)". Remember it totals a block, so treat a pass as a screen, not a verdict on every offer.
