New York City's delivery pay floor

New York City was the first major U.S. city to set a minimum pay standard for app-based food delivery workers. The standard is set by the city's Department of Consumer and Worker Protection (DCWP) and adjusts every April 1.

The rate

Minimum pay = $22.13 × hours of trip time (before tips), effective April 1, 2026

"Trip time" is the time you're actively working a delivery — from accepting it through completing it. Time spent logged in and waiting for an offer isn't counted. The $22.13 is a floor for that trip time, and tips are on top: they belong to you and don't count toward the minimum.

Who it covers

The standard applies to app-based delivery workers making deliveries in New York City for covered delivery apps. It's a delivery rule — rideshare drivers in New York operate under a different state framework, so this page (and the NYC option in our floor check) is specifically about delivery work in the five boroughs.

How to sanity-check your own pay

  1. From your app's earnings summary, take a pay period's trip-time hours and your pay before tips for the same period.
  2. Multiply trip-time hours by $22.13. Example: 20 trip hours × $22.13 = a $442.60 floor.
  3. Compare. If your before-tip pay for that period is below the floor, look for an adjustment on your statement — platforms account for the standard through their pay calculations — and if the gap remains unexplained, DCWP is the agency that enforces the standard and takes worker complaints.

Keep two things in mind. First, because the standard is built on trip time, your pay per total hour online will look lower than $22.13 — that's arithmetic, not necessarily a violation. Second, the rate changes every April, so a check using last year's rate will give you the wrong floor.
