Prop 22 explained: California's earnings guarantee

Proposition 22 (2020) is the California law that keeps app-based drivers and delivery workers classified as independent contractors — and, in exchange, guarantees a minimum level of pay for the time you're actively working. Here's the guarantee in plain language, with 2026 numbers.

The formula

Guaranteed minimum = (120% × minimum wage × engaged hours) + ($0.37 × engaged miles)

For 2026, California's statewide minimum wage is $16.90, so the time part pays at least $20.28 per engaged hour (120% × $16.90). The mileage part is $0.37 per engaged mile for 2026 — an amount adjusted annually under figures published through the State Treasurer. If your city or county minimum wage is higher than $16.90, the guarantee uses your local wage instead.

"Engaged" is the word that matters

The guarantee only covers engaged time: for delivery, roughly from accepting an offer until you complete it; for rideshare, while you're en route to and carrying a passenger. Time spent online waiting for an offer doesn't count toward the guarantee. This is why two drivers with the same hours can have very different guarantees — and why your per-total-hour rate is always lower than your per-engaged-hour rate.

Tips are extra

Tips are yours on top of the guarantee. A platform can't count your tips toward meeting the Prop 22 minimum — if your non-tip pay falls short of the guarantee, the platform owes the difference.

How the top-up actually reaches you

Platforms generally don't re-price each offer to the guarantee in real time. They total your engaged time and miles over a pay period (commonly two weeks), compare your earnings against the guarantee, and add a guarantee adjustment if you came up short. So the place to verify Prop 22 is your earnings statement for the whole period — look for that adjustment line — not any single offer.

Prop 22 also includes a quarterly healthcare stipend for drivers who average enough engaged hours per week and are enrolled in a qualifying health plan. If you're anywhere near full-time engaged hours in California, it's worth checking whether you qualify.

A worked example (2026 rates, statewide wage)

Say that over a pay period you logged 20 engaged hours and 300 engaged miles, and the platform paid you $450 before tips:

  • Time part: $20.28 × 20 = $405.60
  • Mileage part: $0.37 × 300 = $111.00
  • Guarantee: $516.60
  • Shortfall: $516.60 − $450 = $66.60 owed as a top-up

If you were paid $550 before tips instead, you'd be above the guarantee and no adjustment would be due.
