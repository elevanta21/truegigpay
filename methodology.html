Methodology

Every number True Gig Pay produces comes from the formulas and official rates on this page. Nothing is guessed, and no platform pay rate is ever invented — platforms don't publish rate cards, so the calculator works only from totals you enter.

Rates last verified: October 22, 2026.

True hourly rate

Gross $/hr = gross pay ÷ total hours
Net = gross pay − vehicle cost
True net $/hr = net ÷ total hours (and, if you provide it, ÷ engaged hours)

Total hours is all the time you worked, including waiting between offers. Engaged hours is time actively on a delivery or trip. Both figures matter: apps advertise something close to the engaged number, while your life is paid at the total-hours number. Pay laws, below, are written on engaged time.

Vehicle cost — IRS standard mileage mode

The IRS standard mileage rate is its all-in estimate of what a mile of driving costs (fuel, maintenance, tires, insurance, depreciation). For tax year 2026 the rate is not one number but two:

PeriodRateSource
Jan 1 – Jun 30, 202672.5¢ per mileIRS Notice 2026-10 / IR-2025-128; IRS "Standard mileage rates" page
Jul 1 – Dec 31, 202676¢ per mileIRS mid-year increase, IR-2026-29

If your work period sits inside one half of the year, all miles use that half's rate. If it crosses July 1, the calculator splits your miles in proportion to how many of the period's days fall on each side of July 1. That's an approximation — for taxes, what matters is when each mile was actually driven — but it's the honest estimate available from period totals. More: the 2026 two-rate mileage guide.

Vehicle cost — actual-cost mode

Fuel cost = (miles ÷ your MPG) × fuel price per gallon
Vehicle cost = fuel cost + other costs you enter

This mode counts only what you tell it. It will usually be lower than the IRS rate because it leaves out depreciation, insurance, and wear unless you add them under "other costs."

Self-employment tax estimate

Taxable base = 92.35% × net profit (gross − vehicle cost)
Social Security part = 12.4% × taxable base, but only until your year's Social Security wages reach $184,500
Medicare part = 2.9% × taxable base (no cap)
SE tax = the two parts added

This follows the IRS Schedule SE method: the 15.3% combined rate applies to 92.35% of net self-employment income, and the 12.4% Social Security portion stops at the annual wage base — $184,500 for 2026 (Social Security Administration). If you enter W-2 or other SE wages for the year, the calculator reduces the Social Security portion accordingly. There is no SE tax when net profit is zero or negative.

"Set aside" figures scale this period's SE tax to a week (× 7 ÷ period days) and to a quarter (× 91.25 ÷ period days) so you can pace quarterly estimated payments. They assume this period is typical for you.

What this module does not do: it does not estimate your income tax. That depends on filing status, deductions (including the deduction for half of SE tax), other income, and credits — too much of your personal tax picture for a no-data-collection tool. The take-home summary is therefore labeled "before income tax." Quarterly estimated payments normally cover both SE tax and income tax, so most drivers set aside more than the SE figure alone; a tax professional can pin down your percentage.

Offer quick-check

Gross $/hr = offer pay ÷ (minutes ÷ 60)
$/mile = offer pay ÷ miles
Net $/hr = (offer pay − miles × IRS rate for today's date) ÷ (minutes ÷ 60)

The verdict line bands the net $/hr: $25+ "strong," $18–$25 "decent," $12–$18 "marginal," under $12 "poor." Those bands are this site's rough guide, disclosed here so you can judge them — the $/hr and $/mile figures are the objective part.

Limitations

  • Your inputs are the ceiling. If your miles or hours are off, every result is off. The tool cannot read your platform account.
  • Mileage splitting is proportional, not a per-day driving log. Keep a real mileage log for taxes.
  • Floors are approximations at block level. Platforms apply Prop 22 over pay periods with top-up adjustments, NYC's standard through their trip-time accounting, and Seattle/WA per offer or trip. A block below the floor is a prompt to examine your pay statement, not proof of a violation.
  • Local rules vary. A California city minimum wage above $16.90 changes the Prop 22 result (you can enter it); other local ordinances (e.g., other cities' delivery rules) are not yet covered.
  • Rates change on a known calendar: IRS each December (and rarely mid-year, as in 2026), NYC each April 1, California and Washington each January 1. Pages carry a "verified" date so you can tell how fresh the constants are.
